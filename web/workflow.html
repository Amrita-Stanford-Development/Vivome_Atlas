      Step 1

      Two kinds of data

      VivOME learns cell types from RNA. Its reference is   blood cells from Tabula Sapiens, each labelled as one of   types.

      The types nest: each belongs to a group, such as T cell, and each group to a lineage, such as lymphoid. When the evidence is thin, an answer can stop at the group or the lineage instead of guessing a type.

      Proteomics is the other side. Single-cell mass spectrometry measures a few thousand proteins per cell, with many blanks, and no protein label trains anything. The protein datasets decide which genes the model reads, and the development sets test it.

      Step 2

      What the files look like

      A DIA-NN report, like PBMC240's, has one row per protein group and one column per run. It has   rows and   runs, and   of its values are blank: that protein was not seen in that cell.

      The column headers are the instrument's file paths. The service keeps the run's name, so each column becomes one cell.

      Other files arrive the other way round, or already transformed. SCoPE2's published matrix has cells in rows and is centred and batch-corrected; Fulcher 2026's protein table holds abundances on a linear scale. The service reads each as it comes.

      Step 9

      From a point to probabilities

      Each type has a centre on the same sphere, the average point of its reference cells. The closer the cell's point is to a centre, the higher its cosine with it.

      Each encoder turns its cosines into probabilities with a softmax at its own temperature, fitted on held-out RNA cells. A lower temperature makes them sharper.

      The five encoders' probabilities are averaged. The answer is built from this mean.

      Step 10

      The best guess

      Every cell with enough genes also gets a best guess, even when its answer stops short. It joins two views: the encoders' probability, and the cell's correlation with each type's average profile over the genes it measured.

      Step 13

      How the results were calculated, and new data today

      First, simulated uploads: reference cells held out from training, masked like real proteomics and mixed into uploads of known make-up. Answers were right   of the time when given, and   of cells got no answer.

      Second, the benchmark: every method gets the same reference, genes and scoring, and each gap is tested by a paired bootstrap that resamples cells and rescores both methods. On Fulcher 2026 the best guess scores   balanced accuracy, in-sample.

      Third, an internal held-out test: six sets put aside before development and scored once. It passed on held-out donors of another blood atlas and on a T-cell line beside HeLa, failed on two sets, and two were void. It is internal, never external validation.
