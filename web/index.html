The problem

Protein data rarely comes labelled.

Single-cell proteomics measures the proteins that do a cell's work, but few references name the cells it measures.

Why protein cells arrive unnamed

Labelled references for single-cell proteomics are few, and every lab detects a different set of proteins. So a protein measurement of a single cell often arrives without a name.

Single-cell RNA is different: it already has large references, with every cell named.

Your data

Label your own data.

From a matrix to an answer for every cell, in five steps.

  1. 01

    Bring a matrix

    Proteins or genes in rows, cells in columns: a CSV or TSV file, a DIA-NN report, or a FragPipe TMT-Integrator table. Gene symbols, Ensembl and UniProt identifiers are recognised.

  2. 02

    Align

    Features are matched to the reference's   genes, and linear intensities are moved to a log scale. Ambiguous identifiers are reported, never guessed, and cells with too few observed genes are refused.

  3. 03

    Place

    Each cell is placed beside the reference cells it most resembles, by   encoders answering together.

  4. 04

    Answer

    Each cell gets one answer, at the level the evidence supports: a cell type (classical monocyte), its group (monocyte or macrophage), its lineage (myeloid), or no answer with the reason.

  5. 05

    Download

    Every cell comes back with its answer, a set of plausible labels, a confidence, a best guess and its place in the atlas, in one file. The same file always gives the same answer.

The service runs online, in Google's cloud. Send your file from the Your data tab of the Atlas page, and your cells appear on the map with their answers. You can also run your own copy of the service.

Checked against real data.

Against established methods

scANVI, MaxFuse, scGLUE, Harmony and Seurat, plain PCA, and a simple correlation to each class mean, under one protocol with the same reference and the same genes. On data no method was developed on, VivOME's best guess is level with the best of them and ahead of the rest.
