## What B4 is, in plain words

**Where it fits.** Phase B builds the automatic raw-or-log detector, for
uploaders who don't know whether their protein table holds raw (linear) or
logged values. The steps:
- B0 wrote the rules;
- B1 measured what each mistake costs;
- B2 built the detector on the bank of known tables;
- B3 gathered fresh tables with known answers;
- B4 sets the thresholds on half of them (this step);
- B5 to B7 wire it in, check the assembly, and score the other half once
  before it goes live.

**What it does.** For each table the detector gives each reading (linear,
log) a probability. A reading is in the answer when its probability
reaches its threshold. B4 sets each threshold on the calibration tables, so
that a right reading is left out at most 5% of the time, with 95%
confidence. One threshold is set per reading and per size group (stratum).
It also builds the package around the method: `valuescale.detect` and
`apply`, the report they return, the baselines other tools use, and the
test's one-time scorer.

**Why the score changed first.** On the design data, before any
calibration score, B2's score (the smaller of a typicality rank and the
probability) was 0 for any table farther from the design tables than all
of them. With 68 tables in S2+S3, the threshold is the lowest calibration
score, so a threshold of 0 was near certain. That would have put the
reading in every answer, and the guarantee would have said nothing. The
owner chose "probability only" (protocol amendment 5): the answer uses the
probability alone, and typicality becomes a warning that never removes a
reading. A table with no missing value now gets "can't tell", since it may
have been filled in. This run bears the finding out: 36 linear calibration
deposits in S1 and 15 of 68 in S2+S3 have a typicality rank of 0
(B4_typicality_zero).

## The design part, and the owner's choice

The design part reads the bank alone (no calibration or test deposit).

- **B4_model passes:** B2's design rows (89 rows from 18 deposits) refitted
  give B2's model exactly.
- **B4_no_gaps fails.** The rule's condition holds on
  furtwangler_log2_imputed alone, as expected. Among B2's 691 variants it
  also decides one variant of another table:
  furtwangler_log2/per_protein_median.

  - The per-protein rule cannot see it. Most of a protein's values tie at

  variants tested nothing new. The calibration's subsamples, the design rows

The owner chose (2026-11-07) **"Accept, fix, calibrate"**:
- the extra firing is accepted;
- the fault was fixed test first
  (`test_a_table_stored_column_first_gets_the_same_missing_values`, red

The re-run fails the same row in exactly the accepted way. The rule no

## The rehearsal

`python -m valuescale.corpus.score_test --rehearse` ran the test's scorer
on the calibration split (`rehearsal.json`). It checks the code, not the
method: the thresholds were set on these same tables, so in-sample rates
say nothing about the test.
- Every deposit counted once per class and calibrated stratum: 218 in S1,
  68 in S2+S3. No subsample was given the wrong reading alone. In S1, 4
  linear and 5 constructed log subsamples were "unlike either" (k − 1 = 5
  allowed).
- On the whole tables, the detector read all 218 linear tables linear, and
  210 of their constructed logs log (8 linear). Median-50 read 4 linear
  tables log.
- The baselines on the constructed logs: scGPT reads 203 of 218 linear, and
  CellTypist 198; their rules look for log1p of small values. Gemma gives
  no answer on 116. The other rules read them all right.
- The foreign tables: z-scored per protein, all 218 log by the negatives
  gate; presence, all "unlike either"; percentile ranks, 185 "can't tell"
  and 33 log.
- Grouped by software and by instrument family. Nothing was written to
  `valuescale/corpus/test_scored.json`.
