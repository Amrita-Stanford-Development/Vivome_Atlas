## What B7 is, in plain words

- **The detector** (`research/value-scale-plan.md`) reads a protein table and
  answers with a set of readings:
  - linear ("take the log");
  - log ("use as given");
  - both ("can't tell");
  - neither ("unlike either").
- **Its guarantee** (`research/benchmark/protocol-value-scale.md`): for each
  true class and each cell-count stratum, the chance that the true class is
  missing from the set is at most 5%. That holds with 95% confidence over
  the draw of the calibration set.
- **The sealed test** checks the guarantee on 237 public deposits the
  detector had never scored:
  - each deposit's table, which is linear;
  - its constructed log variant.
  A miss is a set without the true class ("wrong" or "unlike either").
- **It was scored once.** `valuescale/corpus/test_started.json` was written
  before any test table was read, and `test_scored.json` after.
  `score_test.py` refuses to run while they exist.

## The result in brief

- **The guarantee is contradicted for log tables of 20 to 49 cells (S1).**
  The detector missed 22 of 237, a one-sided lower bound of 0.064, above
  0.05.
- **It holds elsewhere:**
  - linear tables of 20 to 49 cells: 12 of 237, lower bound 0.030;
  - linear tables of 50 cells or more (S2+S3): 0 of 67;
  - log tables of 50 cells or more: 5 of 67, lower bound 0.030.
- **The primary hypothesis is not supported.** On the 237 linear tables, the
  detector misread 1 and median-50 misread 4. The exact one-sided McNemar
  p is 0.125, against α 0.05.
- **On the whole log tables, the detector reads 215 of 237 as log, and
  median-50 reads 235.** The service reads an "unlike either" table as
  linear (B1's rule), so it would take the log of the other 22 again.
- **Six of the eleven rows pass.**

## What the misses are (post hoc, `posthoc.md`)

### Three test tables the corpus should not have kept

The probe scanned all 457 kept tables, in both splits. It looked for tables
with no observed value, and for tables labelled linear whose values all sit
below 64 (99th percentile). It found three, all in the test split:

- **PXD022054 (MaxQuant) and PXD047898 (FragPipe) are empty.** The columns
  read hold no observed value: 371 rows by 32 columns, and 219 by 36.
  - Neither the detector ("no spread", so "unlike either") nor any
    baseline has anything to read.
  - The walk kept them because its checks read headers and layout, not
    whether any value is observed.
  - Each counts as a miss for both classes.
- **PXD066316 (Spectronaut) is a log2 table labelled linear.**
  - The labelling rule takes Spectronaut's `PG.Quantity` as the quantity
    before the log. This report's values run from 7.96 to 26.76, with a
    median of 15.02, so it wrote log2 values in that column.
  - The detector read it log, as did six of the nine baselines. So the
    test's one wrong linear answer is a wrong label.
  - The test did hold one real log table.
- **No calibration table has either fault.**

**With the three set aside (post hoc):**
- S1 linear: 9 misses of 234, lower bound 0.020.
- S1 log: 20 misses of 234, lower bound 0.057. **Still contradicted.**
- The primary comparison:
  - the detector misreads none of the 234 linear tables;
  - median-50 misreads one, PXD057989, a linear table on a small scale
    (values 0.562 to 153,000, median 29);
  - p = 0.5.

### The log tables the detector cannot place have many missing values

- **Every one of the 20 remaining S1 log misses is on a table with half or
  more of its values missing.** Below that, none of the 146 test tables
  and none of the 138 calibration tables falls under the threshold.
- **The detector's log probability falls as the missing share rises.**
  The Spearman correlation is −0.84 in calibration and −0.85 in the test.
- **The two splits part where 60 to 80% of the values are missing.** Below
  the threshold:
  - calibration: 2 of 36 tables;
  - test: 13 of 41.

  Overall, 5 of 214 against 20 of 234, a one-sided Fisher p of 0.003. That
  comparison was chosen after the result. The whole distributions agree:
  KS p = 0.57.
- **I found no difference in how the two splits were handled:**
  - the split is a hash of the accession;
  - the rehearsal reproduces every calibration score (283 of 283);
  - the audit drew 20% of each split by the same rule;
  - the deposits the clean-up added do not carry the excess: 3 misses of
    24, against 19 of 213 for the rest.
- **What I take from it.**
  - The S1 log threshold (0.931) is the 6th smallest of 218 calibration
    scores. The five under it all come from the 76 calibration tables with
    half or more of their values missing.
  - Either the calibration draw was unlucky (the guarantee allows that with
    probability up to 5%), or something differs that I have not found.
  - Either way, the method's weak point is a log table with many missing
    values. B4 saw it on the bank ("the sparse-log gap",
    `research/notebook-outputs/value_scale_b4/notes.md`). The test shows it
    is wider than calibration implied: on such tables, the threshold does
    not keep the miss rate under 5%.

## What it means for an uploader

- **When the uploader states the reading, nothing here changes.** B5's
  pop-up asks whether the file is raw or log, and a stated reading is
  used.
- **When the uploader does not know, and the table is a log table with
  many missing values,** the detector often cannot place it:
  - it answers "unlike either";
  - the service reads the table as linear (B1's rule), so it takes the log
    again;
  - it warns that neither reading is as clear as on the calibration
    tables.

  On the test, 20 of the 234 sound whole log tables went this way. Today's
  median-50 rule reads all 234 as log. The site's uploads are single-cell
  tables, where many missing values are common.
- **On linear tables, the detector does as well as median-50 or better.**
  On the 234 sound linear tables it read every one linear. Median-50 read
  one as log: a linear table on a small scale, the kind the detector was
  built to catch.
